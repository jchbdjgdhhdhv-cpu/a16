The second guide

Setting the Table

Where the forks go, which glass is whose and where the napkin sits. A calm, logical system you only need to learn once.

Premier

Outside in

Lay cutlery in the order it will be used, from the outside towards the plate. Forks go on the left, knives and spoons on the right, with knife blades facing the plate. Guests simply work inwards, course by course.

Deuxième

The plate and the napkin

Place the dinner plate in the centre, about two fingers from the table edge. The napkin goes either on the plate or to its left, beside the forks. A folded napkin on the plate looks formal; to the left is relaxed and practical.

Troisième

Glasses and water

  • Glasses sit above the knives, on the right.
  • The water glass is placed directly above the dinner knife.
  • Additional glasses line up diagonally to its right, in order of use.

Quatrième

Bread and dessert

A small bread plate sits above the forks, with its butter knife laid across it. Dessert cutlery can rest horizontally above the plate, the spoon handle pointing right and the fork handle pointing left.

Cinquième

Relaxed versus formal

For a casual supper, set only what you need: one fork, one knife, a glass and a napkin. For a formal dinner, add the courses in order. Whatever the style, consistency across every place is what looks elegant.
